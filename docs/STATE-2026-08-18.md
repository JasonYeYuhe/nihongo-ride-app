## The corpus

6,738 of 7,071 entries have an example sentence (95%); 6,724 of those are typeable.
1,004 of the typeable ones are withheld from DICTATION (the voice was measured to read a
kanji in them differently from the way the corpus writes it), leaving 5,720 — no level
below 80%. 64 cards carry a reading note naming the everyday reading of their spelling.

Each sentence carries `exJP`, `exEN`, `exZH`, `exKana` (the typing target, no punctuation —
romaji cannot produce 。), `exTokens` (surface/reading pairs for furigana, reconstructing both
exactly), and `exMeta` recording the model, the reviewer, and **the sense the sentence was
accepted as teaching**.

### The floor: 224 entries no sentence can teach (one was appealed and won)

`docs/measurements/entries-no-sentence-can-teach.json`. Three kinds:

- **98** teach a minority reading whose common counterpart has its own entry — 鼠 ねず beside
  ねずみ, 火傷 かしょう beside やけど. **92 of those siblings already ship a sentence**, so the
  word is taught, just not by that card.
- **118** teach a reading that differs from the default for the spelling, with no sibling.
- **9** failed two independent generation rounds for the identical stated reason. Three of
  those are headwords that are **not valid Japanese spellings**: 擽ぐったい duplicates the stem
  of 擽ったい, 引受る cannot yield ひきうける (needs 引き受ける), ぺん is a loanword in hiragana
  where only ペン occurs.

The rule that ended the retry loop: an entry failing two independent rounds with the same
defect is blocked, not unlucky. Regenerating cannot help, because the generator and the
reading gate share the assumption these entries violate — that a spelling has one likely
reading — so the failure is invisible from inside the pipeline.

**The rule survived an appeal in v1.22 and came out narrower.** v1.21 §A produced a SECOND
parser — Apple's Japanese speech front-end, a different implementation that can be asked
decisively — so "the pipeline cannot judge itself" stopped being the end of the argument.
Six entries got a hearing (`scripts/check_forces_reading.py`,
`docs/measurements/v122-blocked-entry-rescue.json`): **one was rescued** (未だ/いまだ, because
未だに is a fixed form that excludes まだ grammatically — *まだに is not a word), one looked
rescued and was not (違える: the audio proved the reading, but the sentence used 寝違える, a
different verb that merely contains the headword), and four stayed blocked — two of them now
on two parsers disagreeing rather than one tool's verdict.

So the rule is a shipping gate, not a claim about the language, and an appeal now costs one
script run. The other 216 were NOT retried: their blocking reason is the structural one, and
the rescue worked for a grammatical reason peculiar to that entry.

**Decided in v1.21 §C.** They keep their readings. Codex and Gemini were asked again and both
said retire; both argued that the card asks "given this spelling, what reading?" and these
leave it underdetermined. The app never asks that — every mode prints the reading beside the
spelling, so the learner types what they can see. 87 of them gained a note naming the everyday
reading of their spelling and the card that teaches it; the other kind of note was built and
cut for having no second source. Three headwords were resolved as data: ぺん retired toward the
N5 ペン it duplicated, 擽ぐったい and 引受る corrected in `surface` only (`kana` frozen, so the
SM-2 answer never moved).

In-place `kana` edits remain refused, for the original reason: `SRSCard.id` IS `VocabEntry.id`,
so editing kana preserves a learner's schedule while silently swapping the answer being tested.
`check_vocab_diff.py` freezes `kana`, and now refuses a manifest-declared `surface` fix that
tries to move `kana` alongside it.

**A live bug came out of checking whether a retirement was safe.** `resolves:` — the filter that
stops a retired entry counting toward work that no longer exists — was wired into ONE of the
five places that count due cards. The badge, the widget, the reminder body and the journal
forecast counted every stored card, so the two entries v1.18 retired have been inflating them
ever since for anyone who studied them. Fixed in v1.21 §C, with a test on each call site.

## The pipeline

    scripts/gen_batch.py            generate (agy → gemini-3.6-flash), declares a sense per sentence
    scripts/pilot_gate.py           deterministic gates; three-way survive / vocabularyGap / reject
    scripts/make_review_chunks.py   interleaves survivors and gaps, WITHHOLDS the routing verdict
    scripts/apply_batch.py          merge, requires --approved, carries the sense into exMeta
    scripts/gen_sentence_kana.py    exKana + exTokens, with round-trip and alignment gates
    scripts/check_vocab_diff.py     identity frozen, meanings append-only, examples write-once
    scripts/check_translation_agreement.py   en/zh subject and day-part disagreement
    scripts/pipeline_status.py      where a batch stands

`check_vocab_diff` supports `--manifest`: a reviewed structural change (a removal, a rewrite,
a retirement) declares every id and field it touches, and the guard then fails on anything the
manifest did NOT predict **and** on anything it promised that did not happen. It is stricter
with a manifest, not looser.

Review shape that has held for six batches: three lenses (Japanese correctness, translation
fidelity, "would this card mislead a learner") over interleaved chunks, then a sense-triage
with 3–8 independent classifiers and an adversary whose default is to refuse.

## What v1.21 measured about the synthesizer

Kyoko is deterministic, so two texts rendering to the same WAV bytes were converted to the
same phonemes — byte-identity is PROOF of what she said, not an inference. It is decisive and
quiet: it speaks for 521 of 6,723 sentences, of which 494 match their `exKana` and **27 are
proven to say something else** (私 as わたし, 風車 as ふうしゃ, 何 as なに …). A nearest-hypothesis
pass covers the rest and scores **100% recall / 0% false positives** against those 521 labelled
sentences. 1,003 sentences (14.9%) are withheld from dictation; they remain fully available in
sentence mode.

The calibration is the part to remember. The first one used synthetic decoys — a reading
replaced by random kana — and put the false-positive rate at 4.7%, which made a working
instrument look like mostly noise. Random kana are a HARDER test than real alternative
readings. Calibrating on the wrong population is its own way of getting a wrong number from a
clean-looking run.

## The one class of defect this project keeps hitting

Seventeen instances now, and they are all the same sentence: **the number shown and the run
produced are computed by different predicates.**

  v1.12  `dueCards`/`dueCount` counted every stored card while the run dropped ids that no
         longer resolve. Fixed by injecting a `resolves:` closure.
  v1.15  `startGame` had no build-then-guard, so an exhausted level flashed a results screen
         congratulating the learner on 100% accuracy over zero keystrokes.
  v1.22  That same `resolves:` had been wired into ONE of five counting paths — the badge,
         the widget, the reminder body and the journal forecast all still counted withdrawn
         entries, for three releases, uncorrectably.
  v1.22  The weak-words cram gated on `reviewedCount` while the cram itself dropped
         unresolvable ids.
  v1.22  The conjugation half of the badge, same shape, found only because a reviewer looked.
  v1.23  The streak number credited both days of a ride crossing midnight; the strip of studs
         beside it lit only the end day. The card contradicted itself on screen.
  v1.23  `PracticeView` never paused the RunClock, so the Ride Log counted backgrounded time
         as riding — against a comment asserting it measured ridden time.
  v1.23  The coach printed a 200-capped occurrence count as a total, against a comment saying
         the overflow existed so it could say the sample was partial.
  v1.23  The weak-words cram capped at fifteen and filtered after — and withdrawn entries are
         permanent leeches, so they sort to the FRONT of the fifteen. Fifteen promised, twelve
         ridden.
  v1.23  Due-conjugation review, same shape, latent.
  v1.23  `startGame`'s empty-pool notice renders only on the menu, but the results screen calls
         `startGame` too — so "ride again" at an exhausted level did nothing at all.
  v1.24  The Stats screen's conjugation card counted forms practised and "tough forms" over
         cards whose VERB had been withdrawn, while the vocabulary half of the same card had
         been filtered since v1.21 — one screen disagreeing with itself. `leeches()` is the
         worse half: a leech whose verb is gone can never be reviewed away, so the learner is
         shown work they cannot finish. 言う/ゆう, one of the two entries v1.18 retired, is a
         verb. Found by removing the `resolves:` defaults, NOT by the rule written to catch
         this: `ResolvesCallSiteTests` matched `.name(` — with a parenthesis — and these were
         an unfiltered public *property* and a no-argument function.
  v1.24  The chip said 読み and meant the noun "reading", not the 読む the learner had typed —
         and た meant the entry "past tense marker" in 2,695 sentences. The rule guaranteed
         "written and read exactly like this entry", which is TRUE and is weaker than "is this
         entry": an inflected form is written and read exactly like its homographic lemma.
         Found by the pre-submission review; the corpus test that should have caught it had
         re-derived the index's own key and could not fail.
  v1.24  `reviewedCount`, `leeches` and `weakestFormCards` on the conjugation store took a
         filter that nothing exercised: swapping `sourceID` for `id` left 503 tests green while
         production would have shown every learner "Forms practiced 0" forever.

Three lessons hold across all of them. **A fix applied to one call site is not a fix** — five
of the first eight were the same repair left half-done. **The count and the run must share a
predicate, not agree by inspection**: every one of these passed review at the time because
both sides looked right in isolation. And the cheapest detector found so far: **where a
comment states a contract, check whether anything enforces it.** Three of v1.23's six were
found exactly that way — the code had a sentence in it claiming a property nothing checked.

What is now in place against it, and what is not:

  * `VocabStore.resolvesID` is the one predicate. It replaced fifteen hand-written copies, of
    which two were the bugs above. This removes writing it DIFFERENTLY.
  * `Tests/VocabKitTests/ResolvesCallSiteTests.swift` reads the shipped source and fails if a
    learner-facing due count omits `resolves:`. This removes FORGETTING it — which is what
    actually happened both times, and cannot be caught at runtime, because a missing argument
    is indistinguishable from a store where everything resolves. It counts the calls it
    inspected and fails below ten, so a scan that reads nothing cannot report "clean".
  * **v1.24 §C made the argument required.** All ten `= { _ in true }` defaults are gone, so
    forgetting it is now a compile error rather than a silently wrong number. Production
    needed none of them — the app compiled unchanged — which was the whole argument: the
    default served only tests, and cost releases in production. Test noise is absorbed by a
    shim per test target (`Tests/*/UnfilteredForTesting.swift`), 42 call sites, none edited.
  * **The scanner now reads declarations too**, because the call-site scan had a blind spot
    that was hiding two live defects, and the compiler cannot catch a counting method that
    never declared the parameter. Any `public func`/`public var` on a `*Store` whose name
    begins due/reviewed/leech/weakest must take `resolves:`. Calibrated on the exact two it
    found, plus the false positive its first version had (`SRSCard.dueDate` — one card's own
    date, where the predicate is meaningless).
  * Neither helps a NEW promise built on a filter that has no keyword. The audit's own
    calibration named this: its rule works because the codebase adopted `resolves:` in
    response to this very defect. For the next novel one, the sweep degenerates to "read
    everything". v1.24 §A's "ride these words" is exactly such a path — no `resolves:`
    keyword at the call site — and it is held instead by a single function
    (`StumbledWords.rideableIDs`) that both the count and the run must call, with a test
    asserting the button's number against the length of the queue the button produces.

## Traps this project has already paid for

- **A test target can damage the user's real data through a door the sandbox does not cover.**
  v1.24 added the first app-layer test target; every `AppModel` it built called
  `refreshWidgetSnapshot`, which writes to the **App Group container** — outside
  `supportFileURL`, and therefore outside `supportDirectoryOverride`. Seven models per
  `swift test`, each computed from an empty sandbox, each stamping fourteen days of zeros onto
  the owner's real home-screen widget. It had already happened before the review found it.
  Two lessons, and the second is the one worth keeping: the sandbox assertion was GREEN, because
  it checked the one door that was known about. The replacement asserts the property — running
  the tests changes nothing that belongs to the user — over every location, so the next escape
  trips it without anyone predicting which door it uses. There are three such locations:
  Application Support, the App Group container, and `UserDefaults`.
- **`Bundle.main.bundleIdentifier != nil` is not "this is the app".** Under XCTest hosting it is
  `com.apple.dt.xctest.tool`, and the App Group container still resolves. The guard works today
  only because the app tests use swift-testing, whose SwiftPM entry point reports nil. Sandbox
  the destination, do not sniff the process.


- **Adding a stored property to a struct in a library target can leave SPM's incremental
  build inconsistent, and it presents as a SIGSEGV in an unrelated test.** v1.24 added one
  field to `VocabStore`, which changed the struct's layout; object files compiled against the
  old layout were not all rebuilt, and `swift test` began crashing with signal 11 inside
  `GameSession.makeWeak` — a v1.23 test that had nothing to do with the change — with the
  crash point moving between runs and the output garbled mid-line. Every symptom points at a
  memory-safety bug in the new code. It is not one. **`rm -rf .build` and rebuild**, and only
  then start diagnosing. Confirmed by bisection: the same tree passed clean after a full
  rebuild, and 486 tests went green. Cost about an hour, most of it spent reading a crash log
  for a bug that did not exist.

- **`swift test` output is not a reliable input to a script.** With a failure present the
  runner can exit on a signal with truncated, interleaved output, so a harness that parses the
  console can see a suite as "not failing" when it never ran at all. Anything automated over
  `swift test` must assert that the test it cares about actually appeared in the run. See the
  mutation harnesses described under v1.24 §A.

- **`try!` on `#require` in a test aborts the process.** One failing app-layer test took twelve
  unrelated ones down with it and left a red build unreadable. Use a `throws` test and plain
  `try #require`, so a failure fails alone.


- **Finder/iCloud duplicate copies appear inside `Sources/` and break the build.** On
  2026-08-18, two hours after v1.23 shipped, `ListsView 2.swift` and `PracticeView 2.swift`
  materialised in `Sources/NihongoRideApp/` carrying their original July 29 mtimes, and
  `swift build` went red with `invalid redeclaration`. The working tree had been clean
  minutes earlier. `.gitignore` is no defence — git ignoring a file does not stop SPM
  compiling it — and neither is `git status` alone, since these arrive untracked. The same
  mechanism produced the seven `NihongoRide N.xcodeproj` copies at the repo root, which are
  harmless only because `*.xcodeproj/` is generated and ignored. If a build fails with
  redeclarations nobody wrote, look for a file with a space and a digit before `.swift`
  before looking anywhere else.

- **A null result from an uncalibrated checker is worthless.** Five near-misses in one session:
  an honesty judge reporting 205/205 that found 1 of 3 known-bad; a card check reporting "9
  flagged, 0 confirmed" because `v && v.flag` turned a missing agent result into a pass; a
  Chinese false-friend sweep finding 3 because it compared Japanese shinjitai to Simplified
  characters. Build the negative control before believing the number.
- **A gate and the thing it gates can share a blind spot.** Sudachi as the reading arbiter
  agreed with the generator's wrong reading 14 times out of 17 — both regress to the common
  reading, which is the failure under examination.
- **One rule written twice will drift.** Three times the Swift test and the Python gate
  disagreed and the Swift copy was the stale one: a fullwidth ？ outside its punctuation set,
  -ずる verbs conjugating on a じ stem, and the i-adjective POS tag written three ways across
  nine entries (recorded here as "nine different spellings", which was the count of entries,
  not of spellings — v1.21 §D collapsed the whole 42-tag vocabulary and put a data test on it).
- **Calibrating on one corpus does not mean the rule holds on another.** The
  translation-agreement check looked clean on 3,812 shipped sentences and was wrong four times
  out of five on the first new batch. Its number-comparison half was eventually **deleted** —
  one real catch against eight false positives over seven attempts to fix it.
