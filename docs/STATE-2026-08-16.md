Each sentence carries `exJP`, `exEN`, `exZH`, `exKana` (the typing target, no punctuation —
romaji cannot produce 。), `exTokens` (surface/reading pairs for furigana, reconstructing both

accepted as teaching**.

### The floor: 224 entries no sentence can teach (one was appealed and won)

`docs/measurements/entries-no-sentence-can-teach.json`. Three kinds:

- **98** teach a minority reading whose common counterpart has its own entry — 鼠 ねず beside
  ねずみ, 火傷 かしょう beside やけど. **92 of those siblings already ship a sentence**, so the
  word is taught, just not by that card.
- **118** teach a reading that differs from the default for the spelling, with no sibling.
- **9** failed two independent generation rounds for the identical stated reason. Three of
  those are headwords that are **not valid Japanese spellings**: 擽ぐったい duplicates the stem
  of 擽ったい, 引受る cannot yield ひきうける (needs 引き受ける), ぺん is a loanword in hiragana
  where only ペン occurs.

The rule that ended the retry loop: an entry failing two independent rounds with the same
defect is blocked, not unlucky. Regenerating cannot help, because the generator and the
reading gate share the assumption these entries violate — that a spelling has one likely
reading — so the failure is invisible from inside the pipeline.

**The rule survived an appeal in v1.22 and came out narrower.** v1.21 §A produced a SECOND
parser — Apple's Japanese speech front-end, a different implementation that can be asked
decisively — so "the pipeline cannot judge itself" stopped being the end of the argument.
Six entries got a hearing (`scripts/check_forces_reading.py`,
`docs/measurements/v122-blocked-entry-rescue.json`): **one was rescued** (未だ/いまだ, because
未だに is a fixed form that excludes まだ grammatically — *まだに is not a word), one looked
rescued and was not (違える: the audio proved the reading, but the sentence used 寝違える, a
different verb that merely contains the headword), and four stayed blocked — two of them now
on two parsers disagreeing rather than one tool's verdict.

So the rule is a shipping gate, not a claim about the language, and an appeal now costs one
script run. The other 216 were NOT retried: their blocking reason is the structural one, and
the rescue worked for a grammatical reason peculiar to that entry.

**Decided in v1.21 §C.** They keep their readings. Codex and Gemini were asked again and both
said retire; both argued that the card asks "given this spelling, what reading?" and these
leave it underdetermined. The app never asks that — every mode prints the reading beside the
spelling, so the learner types what they can see. 87 of them gained a note naming the everyday
reading of their spelling and the card that teaches it; the other kind of note was built and
cut for having no second source. Three headwords were resolved as data: ぺん retired toward the
N5 ペン it duplicated, 擽ぐったい and 引受る corrected in `surface` only (`kana` frozen, so the
SM-2 answer never moved).

In-place `kana` edits remain refused, for the original reason: `SRSCard.id` IS `VocabEntry.id`,
so editing kana preserves a learner's schedule while silently swapping the answer being tested.
`check_vocab_diff.py` freezes `kana`, and now refuses a manifest-declared `surface` fix that
tries to move `kana` alongside it.

**A live bug came out of checking whether a retirement was safe.** `resolves:` — the filter that
stops a retired entry counting toward work that no longer exists — was wired into ONE of the
five places that count due cards. The badge, the widget, the reminder body and the journal
forecast counted every stored card, so the two entries v1.18 retired have been inflating them
ever since for anyone who studied them. Fixed in v1.21 §C, with a test on each call site.

## The pipeline

    scripts/gen_batch.py            generate (agy → gemini-3.6-flash), declares a sense per sentence
    scripts/pilot_gate.py           deterministic gates; three-way survive / vocabularyGap / reject
    scripts/make_review_chunks.py   interleaves survivors and gaps, WITHHOLDS the routing verdict
    scripts/apply_batch.py          merge, requires --approved, carries the sense into exMeta
    scripts/gen_sentence_kana.py    exKana + exTokens, with round-trip and alignment gates
    scripts/check_vocab_diff.py     identity frozen, meanings append-only, examples write-once
    scripts/check_translation_agreement.py   en/zh subject and day-part disagreement
    scripts/pipeline_status.py      where a batch stands

`check_vocab_diff` supports `--manifest`: a reviewed structural change (a removal, a rewrite,
a retirement) declares every id and field it touches, and the guard then fails on anything the
manifest did NOT predict **and** on anything it promised that did not happen. It is stricter
with a manifest, not looser.

Review shape that has held for six batches: three lenses (Japanese correctness, translation
fidelity, "would this card mislead a learner") over interleaved chunks, then a sense-triage
with 3–8 independent classifiers and an adversary whose default is to refuse.

## What v1.21 measured about the synthesizer

Kyoko is deterministic, so two texts rendering to the same WAV bytes were converted to the
same phonemes — byte-identity is PROOF of what she said, not an inference. It is decisive and
quiet: it speaks for 521 of 6,723 sentences, of which 494 match their `exKana` and **27 are
proven to say something else** (私 as わたし, 風車 as ふうしゃ, 何 as なに …). A nearest-hypothesis
pass covers the rest and scores **100% recall / 0% false positives** against those 521 labelled
sentences. 1,003 sentences (14.9%) are withheld from dictation; they remain fully available in
sentence mode.

The calibration is the part to remember. The first one used synthetic decoys — a reading
replaced by random kana — and put the false-positive rate at 4.7%, which made a working
instrument look like mostly noise. Random kana are a HARDER test than real alternative
readings. Calibrating on the wrong population is its own way of getting a wrong number from a
clean-looking run.

## The one class of defect this project keeps hitting

Eight instances now, and they are all the same sentence: **the number shown and the run
produced are computed by different predicates.**

  v1.12  `dueCards`/`dueCount` counted every stored card while the run dropped ids that no
         longer resolve. Fixed by injecting a `resolves:` closure.
  v1.15  `startGame` had no build-then-guard, so an exhausted level flashed a results screen
         congratulating the learner on 100% accuracy over zero keystrokes.
  v1.22  That same `resolves:` had been wired into ONE of five counting paths — the badge,
         the widget, the reminder body and the journal forecast all still counted withdrawn
         entries, for three releases, uncorrectably.
  v1.22  The weak-words cram gated on `reviewedCount` while the cram itself dropped
         unresolvable ids.
  v1.22  The conjugation half of the badge, same shape, found only because a reviewer looked.

Two lessons hold across all of them. **A fix applied to one call site is not a fix** — five
of the eight were the same repair left half-done. And **the count and the run must share a
predicate, not agree by inspection**: every one of these passed review at the time because
both sides looked right in isolation.

## Traps this project has already paid for

- **A null result from an uncalibrated checker is worthless.** Five near-misses in one session:
  an honesty judge reporting 205/205 that found 1 of 3 known-bad; a card check reporting "9
  flagged, 0 confirmed" because `v && v.flag` turned a missing agent result into a pass; a
  Chinese false-friend sweep finding 3 because it compared Japanese shinjitai to Simplified
  characters. Build the negative control before believing the number.
- **A gate and the thing it gates can share a blind spot.** Sudachi as the reading arbiter
  agreed with the generator's wrong reading 14 times out of 17 — both regress to the common
  reading, which is the failure under examination.
- **One rule written twice will drift.** Three times the Swift test and the Python gate
  disagreed and the Swift copy was the stale one: a fullwidth ？ outside its punctuation set,
  -ずる verbs conjugating on a じ stem, and the i-adjective POS tag written three ways across
  nine entries (recorded here as "nine different spellings", which was the count of entries,
  not of spellings — v1.21 §D collapsed the whole 42-tag vocabulary and put a data test on it).
- **Calibrating on one corpus does not mean the rule holds on another.** The
  translation-agreement check looked clean on 3,812 shipped sentences and was wrong four times
  out of five on the first new batch. Its number-comparison half was eventually **deleted** —
  one real catch against eight false positives over seven attempts to fix it.
